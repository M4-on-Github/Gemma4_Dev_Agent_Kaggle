## Takeaway
For two small instruction-tuned models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-Turbo, Llama-3-8B-Instruct-Lite) on GPQA Diamond, majority voting over 64 samples barely moves the aggregate score but lowers per-problem accuracy on more than half of the problems. The proposed cheap gates (answer agreement, token entropy) do not recover the headroom.

## Relevance (mine)
- Voting among samples of a model that is mostly wrong on a question pushes toward its consistent wrong answer. For a proposer whose candidate list may not contain the right fix, a larger N makes the vote more certain of the same candidate, not more correct.
- The paper's conclusion is that a signal from outside the model's own samples is needed; in repair that signal is an executed test, which the one-vote-point design does not have.
- Tasks here are hard knowledge questions with one right letter out of four; our vote is among a proposer's own candidates, so the match is loose.

## Ideas to test
- (mine) Claim: on tasks where the base E4B single loop fails, the 16-voter winner is the proposer's first-listed candidate in at least 80% of cases. If so, the vote reinforces the model's own prior and the screen's useful outcome (D >= 1) comes from noise.

## Caveats
Hard science multiple choice, temperature 0.7, 7B and 8B API-hosted instruct models, N=64, one author. Mechanism in the paper is not given beyond the dispersion of per-problem accuracy.
