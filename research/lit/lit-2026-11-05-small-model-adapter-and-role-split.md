## Takeaway

Q1 (does small-scale SFT or LoRA fix tool-call errors, especially exact-copy arguments). Two sources give a within-model before and after for a format or loop error: ToolACE (LLaMA-3.1-8B, 3-shot base 58.81 against fine-tuned 89.27 on BFCL Non-live AST, App. G Table 9) and SWE-Gym (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, stuck-in-loop 39.6 to 21.0 on Verified). xLAM and Hammer report BFCL scores for the trained model against other models, not against their own base. SWE-smith points the other way on loops: its fine-tuned 32B repeats more than the teacher it learned from. Argument exactness, and exact copy of shown text: no source I opened measures it. The nearest datum is one fine-tuning pilot on a 1.5B model in a withdrawn paper (0 of 33 to 12 to 17% exact match). General-capability loss: only one source quantifies forgetting (Biderman et al., Llama-2-7B), and its table runs the opposite way to "low rank caused the loss".

Q2 (does a role-split pipeline beat a single loop at equal budget at small scale on SWE-style tasks). No measurement found. I found nothing on SWE-style tasks below about 20B. The closest equal-call-budget datum (Qwen2.5-7B, Planner, Executor, Critic, ALFWorld and WebShop, not SWE) shows no significant gain and 1.79 times the calls. The trained-role SWE pipelines (Co-PatcheR, SWE-Fixer) have no single-loop control at equal budget.

### What the sources support (and what they do not)

1. Format and loop errors fall after SFT in two within-model comparisons. ToolACE: 3-shot to fine-tuned Non-live AST 58.81 to 89.27 on an 8B. SWE-Gym: 7B stuck-in-loop 39.6 to 21.0 on Verified from 491 teacher trajectories. xLAM and Hammer show high BFCL scores at 1B to 7B but no own-base row. SWE-smith is evidence the other way for loops: its fine-tuned 32B repeats more than its teacher.
2. Exact-copy arguments are not measured by any opened source. BFCL's AST check is about function names, required parameters and types (per the Hammer extract), not the fidelity of a long string copied from context. The one edit-fidelity datum is the withdrawn 1.5B pilot, 12 to 17% after training on a different task (HEvalFix). So "an adapter makes the model's old string match the file" is untested in the literature I could open.
3. Fixing the interface need not move task success. Existing notes: CODESTRUCT on Qwen3-8B cut empty patches 179 to 138 with pass@1 13.2 to 13.0 (`lit-2604.05407`); Aider's page, via summary, says small open models gain almost nothing from a format change (`lit-aider2025-editformats`). SWE-Gym's 32B empty patches rose 9.5 to 13.8 while resolve rose 7.0 to 20.6, so the two rates move independently.
4. Data origin that worked: synthetic and verified (xLAM, ToolACE: executed or rule-checked) and teacher-trajectory filtered by success (SWE-Gym 491, SWE-smith 5,016). xLAM's Fig. 5 reports that training on data that failed the semantic or execution checks hurt, more for the 1B model (direction only, no values in the text). No source used a model's own failures as targets with a positive result; SWE-Gym's extractor-reported on-policy mix lowered 32B Lite 15.3 to 8.7.
5. Ranks and sizes actually reported: ToolACE r=16, alpha 32 on 8B; SWE-Gym 32B rank 64 (second scaffold); Biderman r=16, 64, 256 on 7B. There is no sweep of rank for tool-call accuracy. Biderman shows that at instruction-tuning scale (110K examples), r=16 under-learns code (HumanEval 0.358 against 0.470 full) while retaining the most outside knowledge. xLAM, Hammer and SWE-Gym state no general-capability check; SWE-smith's is inside SWE-bench only; ToolACE's has no numbers in text.
6. The C-013 comparison (rank-3 adapter on broad SWE trajectories lost 10.8 points). Biderman's forgetting values do not support reading that loss as a rank effect: lower rank retained more (0.609 at r=16 against 0.414 full). The C-013 loss is a drop in the target task, not outside-domain forgetting, so Biderman does not measure it directly. (Mine) The closer published analogs are training-target effects: SWE-smith's repeated-action behaviour after training on teacher trajectories, and SWE-Gym's on-policy self-improvement result. Neither is proven to be the cause of C-013.

What the sources support:
1. No source I opened gives a measured gain of a role split over a single loop at equal budget on SWE-style tasks below about 20B. The only small-model equal-call-budget comparison (Dylan et al.) found no significant gain (+0.015, p=0.80) on a non-SWE benchmark, and the team cost 1.79 times the calls even under a nominally equal search budget.
2. Positive role-split results are trained roles (alpha-UMi, Co-PatcheR, ProST). Co-PatcheR's own one-model-versus-three comparison on localise plus generate found them similar, while separate validators did better (numbers cut off).
3. The single-loop-against-workflow gap in Seed-Coder Table 15 is large for models without agentic training, but it is a fixed workflow of sampled calls, not budget-matched, and Qwen2.5-Coder-32B's 30.2 against 5.6 shows the loop result depends on training for tool loops, not on size (existing note).
4. A pipeline that ends each stage by removing tools is not tested in any source. Dylan et al. report that planner and critic prompts evolved to empty and the critic's influence on the chosen action was 0.41 and the planner's 0.14, so in that setup the prompted roles were close to inert. That is the only evidence about whether short prompted roles do anything on a 7B.

## Relevance

Transfers to our constraints: Q1 partly. The harness lets an adapter in (rank up to 128) but no source tells us the E4B's exact-copy failure is fixable by SFT; the fit is on format and loop errors. Q2: negative prior, not a refutation, because none of the tests were SWE tasks with tools removed per stage.

For D-006:
- The "narrow, exact-copy adapter" line has literature support for format and loop failure (two before and after comparisons) and none for exact-copy fidelity. The pair construction in D-006 (a window of a file plus an exact unique substring) resembles Hammer's idea of making the model read the argument from the prompt (masking forces copying from context), but nobody has measured copying (mine).
- The role-team line has a negative equal-budget prior at 7B (Dylan et al.). The 1.8 times call cost is the number to check against the D-006 kill criterion that the editor is reached on at least 6 of 8 tasks.

## Ideas to test

- (mine) Claim: on smoke-8 with the E4B, `SequentialAgent[locate, edit, check]` uses at least 1.8 times the model calls per task of the single v24 loop; read from `analyze_traces.py` calls per task for the two arms on the same ids. If it is below 1.2 times, the extra-calls cost does not apply here; if above 3 times, the editor is unlikely to be reached inside the call cap on several of 8 tasks (the cap value for the E4B tree is not taken from any source here; read it from the tree's `eval_config.yaml`).
- (mine) Claim: the E4B role team applies an edit on no more tasks than the single loop (difference under 2 of 8), on the same ids at equal call cap, which is Dylan et al.'s null (+0.015, p=0.80) transferred. A difference of 2 or more tasks of 8 in the team's favour falsifies it.
- (mine) Claim: an adapter trained only on synthetic exact-copy pairs (the unique substring of a shown file window, wrapped as the `edit_file` call) lowers the share of `edit_file` calls whose old string is not found or matches more than once on the EXP-043 failed-edit-8 ids by at least half; the loop and empty-patch rates may fall separately. No source measures this, so a null is informative.
- (mine) Claim: an adapter trained on whole-trajectory data repeats an action three times in a row on a larger share of tasks than the base model (the SWE-smith finding of over 25% of trajectories with a length-10 repeat); measure with the repeat counters in `analyze_traces.py`.
- (mine) Data rule if training ever starts: keep only pairs whose substring occurs exactly once in the window and apply the full tool call to the window to confirm it applies, the analogue of xLAM's format and execution checks; xLAM's Fig. 5 says unverified data hurt, more at the smaller size.

## Caveats

- Teacher-trajectory SFT (SWE-Gym, SWE-smith) used frontier-model trajectories; our adapter would have none and, under plan.md:52, cannot export competition snippets, so only synthetic pairs are available (D-006).
- BFCL and ToolBench are single-call benchmarks; none measures a string copied verbatim from a 30-line window.
- Whether an adapter is applied by the harness's server is Q4 and unresolved; the literature does not bear on it.
- Dylan et al. is a 2026 preprint on non-SWE tasks with one frozen 7B backbone, single greedy evaluation per cell; read only to character 100,000 of 133,741.
- Extractor-reported numbers are one reading of the page; figure-only values (xLAM Fig. 5, ToolACE Fig. 3 to 8, Hammer Fig. 2 and 5) were not available.
- Not found: any result on exact-copy fidelity under SFT or LoRA at small scale; any SWE-style equal-budget role-split below 20B; any forgetting measurement for a tool-call adapter.
