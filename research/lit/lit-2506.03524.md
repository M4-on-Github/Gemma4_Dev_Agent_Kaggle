## Takeaway
The best same-model comparison I found between a fixed workflow and an autonomous loop for sub-35B models: for small and mid-size instruct models, Agentless beats OpenHands, often by a lot. The authors give the reason as capacity, and note one exception.

## Reported results
Table 15, SWE-bench Verified resolve rate (%), Agentless / OpenHands / Multi-SWE-bench mini Agentless:
| Model | Size | Agentless | OpenHands | Multi-SWE mini |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed-Coder-8B-Instruct | 8B | 19.2 | 11.2 | 4.0 |
| Qwen3-8B | 8B | 14.6 | 3.4 | 2.3 |
| Qwen3-8B-Thinking | 8B | 12.5 | 8.6 | 2.5 |
| Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct | 7B | 4.2 | 1.0 | 0.5 |
| Qwen2.5-Coder-14B-Instruct | 14B | 19.4 | 1.4 | 3.8 |
| Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct | 32B | 30.2 | 5.6 | 4.5 |
| QwQ-32B | 32B | 18.4 | 15.8 | 4.5 |

Quote: "This gap is primarily due to the limited capacity of smaller models, which typically rely on pre-defined workflows" (§5.2.4). Agentless is "a fixed, manually designed workflow"; OpenHands "has no requirements of any fixed workflow". Agentless sample counts and OpenHands iteration limits were not in the returned text.

## Relevance
Part E. Evidence that for models without agentic post-training a fixed locate-repair pipeline beats a free loop. Note the 32B rows: Qwen2.5-Coder-32B gets 30.2 under Agentless and 5.6 under OpenHands, so the OpenHands gap is a property of models not trained for tool loops, not of parameter count. Gemma 4 31B and the 4B variant are tool-calling-trained, so this is a prior, not a prediction.

Transfers to our constraints: partly. The Agentless side uses multiple samples and test selection (see lit-2407.01489) that we cannot afford; the lesson that transfers is "narrow, sequenced roles reduce what the model must plan".

## Caveats
Different harnesses per column; OpenHands settings unknown; trained-vs-untrained agent behaviour is a confound.
