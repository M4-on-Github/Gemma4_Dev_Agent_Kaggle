## Takeaway
Tree search over an open-source SWE agent with a model-based value function and a debate-style discriminator improves five models, including three open-weight 70B-class models, by 17 to 27% relative on SWE-bench Lite. The value function converges on the correct solution only 73% of the time.

## Method
- SWE-Agent for exploration, Value Agent giving feedback, Discriminator Agent that debates the final choice; at most 3 expansions per node and 100 search iterations in §4, but Table 2 lists max_expansions 5 (inconsistent) (§4, App. C).
- Whether value and discriminator use the same model as the actor is not stated in the extraction.

## Reported results
Table 1 (SWE-bench Lite resolve rate, %): Moatless baseline / SWE-Search / relative gain:
| Model | Baseline | SWE-Search | Gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-4o | 24.3 (v0.0.2); 25.7 (adapted) | 31.0 | +17 |
| GPT-4o-mini | 13.0 | 17.0 | +24 |
| Qwen-2.5-72b-Instruct | 18.0 | 24.7 | +27 |
| Deepseek-V2.5 | 16.3 | 21.0 | +22 |
| Llama-3.1-70b-Instruct | 13.6 | 17.7 | +23 |

Mean +23% relative (§4.1.1). Discriminator: "improves final selection accuracy to 84%" (§3.5, §4.2). Value function "converged on the correct solution 73% of the time on average across the five models"; it "sometimes failed to recognize critical decision points" (§4.2). No resolve-rate ablation with and without the discriminator was found in the extraction.

## Relevance
Part A/C/E: a model-as-value-function and debate selector adds measurable score for open 70B-class models, but at 100 search iterations, which is one to two orders more model calls than our cap allows; 73% and 84% are accuracy of picking the right branch, not a resolve-rate gain. Small models under 10B were not tested.

Transfers to our constraints: no on cost (100 iterations vs 28 to 45 calls), partly on the existence of same-family model judges that work for 70B open models.

## Caveats
