## Takeaway
One task (Towers of Hanoi, 20 disks, 2^20 - 1 steps) is split into one-step subtasks; each step is decided by sampling a small non-reasoning model until one answer leads every other by k votes, discarding over-long or badly formatted replies. It works because per-step error is tiny (0.22% to 0.4% for gpt-4.1-mini) and the step carries its whole state in its input. It is not evidence for SWE repair: the only other task is digit multiplication, the step answer is exactly checkable, and no workspace is mutated by an unverifiable action.

## Method
- Voting rule: sample candidates for a subtask until one leads all others by k votes (§3.2, Algorithm 2 `do_voting`); success of one step under the rule is Eq. 9; minimum k for target success t over s steps is `k_min = ceil(ln(t^(-m/s) - 1) / ln((1-p)/p))` = Theta(ln s) (Eq. 14, App. B Eq. 23); expected cost with red-flagging `c * s * k_min / (v(2p-1))` (Eq. 19); with m = 1 (maximal decomposition) cost is Theta(s ln s) (Eq. 17, 18).
- Red-flagging (§3.3, Algorithm 3): discard (resample, never repair) replies that are too long or wrongly formatted. In the final run the output cap is 750 tokens with a strict parser (§4.4); the exploratory runs used 2048 tokens and a repairing parser with no red flag (§4.2).
- Temperature (§4.4): first vote of a step at temperature 0, all later votes at 0.1. At least k_min = 3 votes are sampled in parallel per step (§4.4).
- State is handled by having each agent output the next state, which the next agent receives (§4.1). Exact-match voting (§5).

## Relevance (mine, not the paper's)
- It names the one idea that is not majority voting: a margin stopping rule (lead of k) that spends votes only where they disagree, plus discard-and-resample on malformed replies. Both need sequential sampling; a declarative `ParallelAgent` has fixed N.
- Its voters are nearly deterministic (0, then 0.1), and the vote format is the tiny exact-match answer. That supports the one-letter `PICK: X` format of EXP-039 Q4 and counts against a 0.2 to 1.4 temperature spread as the source of diversity: at tau=1.0 vs 0.1 the same model's error nearly doubled (Fig. 6(b)).
- The only 3B-class datapoint is a total failure (error 1.0). The paper's small-model result is gpt-oss-20B (error .0358, k_min 6), which is a reasoning model. Nothing at 4B.
- The paper's own limits (§5): i.i.d. errors assumed, correlated errors "an open foundational problem", strategy given a priori, "which kinds of tasks are most resistant to such a decomposition" unknown. A repair task has an unscripted strategy and no state-in-the-prompt, so the premise p close to 1 does not hold.

## Ideas to test
- (mine) Claim: in a V1 screen, voter replies longer than 20 tokens or not matching `PICK: [A-D]` exactly are no more likely to be wrong than parseable ones only if the format is the cause; test by counting, per voter temperature bucket (0.2-0.6, 0.6-1.0, 1.0-1.4), the malformed rate; expect the top bucket at least 3 points higher than the bottom, which is the red-flag signal. Zero-GPU after the screen from the trace.
- (mine) Claim: replacing the plurality rule by "apply the top letter only if it leads the runner-up by at least 3 of 16 votes, else voter 1's letter" changes the applied pick relative to plain plurality in at most 1 of 8 smoke tasks. If it changes more, the lead is often small and the vote is a coin flip. Expressible as a sentence in the actor prompt; fidelity of a 4B actor to it is itself F3.

## Caveats
Frontier or large non-reasoning API models, exact-match answers, perfect decomposition prescribed by hand, cost in dollars not wall clock, Hanoi only (plus multiplication). Not read: Appendix D distribution, Appendix F detail, cost of the 20-disk run.
