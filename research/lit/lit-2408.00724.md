## Takeaway
At a fixed inference compute budget, a smaller model sampled many times and voted is more accurate than a larger model sampled once or a few times, until the small model saturates; the cross-over is far above the 8 to 32 sample range. The paper is math reasoning with a trained reward model.

## Method
- Llemma-7B and Llemma-34B (fine-tuned on MetaMath), Mistral-7B, Pythia; Llama3-Instruct-8B in App. D.2; reward model Llemma-34B fine-tuned on Math-Shepherd (§4.1). Temperature 1.0 for all strategies, Rebase balance temperature 0.1 (App. C).

## Relevance (mine)
- For the cost-against-stronger-pass question: this is a cost source only for models where the stronger pass is a larger sibling at matched FLOPs and the answer is checkable by a reward model. Here, the 31B and the E4B are different submissions; we cannot run the 31B inside an E4B budget.
- The number most relevant to N of 8 to 32 is the one 32-sample row: majority voting is 41.0 on MATH500 and a trained verifier adds 2.5 points (43.5), so even on math the selector matters more than the sample count.

## Caveats
