## Takeaway
8B and 14B open models fine-tuned for the Agentless pipeline show how best@k selection and majority voting scale with k on SWE-bench Verified. Majority voting "saturates earlier"; test-based selection keeps gaining but stays far under pass@k.

## Method
- Agentless framework with three phases: localization, repair, patch validation (§3.3.1, §4.7).
- Validation: each candidate runs the repo's existing regression tests, then a curated subset of generated reproduction tests (10 per instance in §3.3.1; "40 reproduction tests per instance" in §7.4); the patch with the highest combined pass rate is selected, called best@k. Ties favour a shorter generation or smaller edit distance (§3.3.1).
- Context: SFT sequence 32K; evaluation input 32K (8B) and 64K (14B) with YaRN scaling (§4.7).

## Relevance
Open small-model data for part A: majority voting adds almost nothing past a few samples, and even test-based selection leaves three quarters of the pass@k headroom. These models are trained for this pipeline, which our Gemma 4 is not.

Transfers to our constraints: partly. Same size class as our small-model case (8B), long contexts (32K to 64K) and k up to 32 with executed tests; none of that fits a 4.5 minute cap.
