Read through the fetch tool on 2026-11-05 (Table 1, Table 2, §3, §5). Per-N Pass@1 values are only in Figure 10 and were not read. The paper's own text contains two different improvement ranges (5.01 to 12.86 in §5.1.2, 5.83 to 14.60 in §1 and §5.2.2).

## Takeaway
Generation, pruning and an LLM selector agent beat majority-voting and other ensemble baselines at N=3 on SWE-bench Verified with frontier models. Useful for the gap between the average candidate, the oracle, and what a selector recovers at a small N.

## Method
- Three components: patch generation, pruning (removes duplicate or regressing patches, "28.90% on average" redundancy, §3.3), selection by a selector agent that reads the repo (§3.4). The selector's model is not stated; the paper uses Claude 3.7 Sonnet as the base for all ensemble techniques (§4.4).
- Interaction capped at 30 rounds for the selector (§3.4). Baselines: Augment, DEI-base (± pruning), Adversary, Average, Oracle.

## Reported results
Table 1, Pass@1, N=3 (rows as printed):
| Technique | Gemini 2.5 Pro | Claude 3.7 Sonnet | GPT-4.1 | Mixture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Average | 53.40% | 61.33% | 51.40% | 56.33% |
| Oracle | 66.20% | 70.00% | 63.00% | 73.40% |
| Augment | 55.40% | 63.13% | 54.87% | 58.93% |
| DEI-base | 53.53% | 62.33% | 53.13% | 55.87% |
| Trae Agent | 62.27% | 66.40% | 59.00% | 65.67% |

| Ablation (Table 2) | Gemini | Claude | GPT-4.1 | Mixture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| without pruning (woP) | 58.40% | 63.60% | 56.20% | 61.80% |
| full | 62.27% | 66.40% | 59.00% | 65.67% |

Other: at ensemble size 10, "Oracle achieves an average improvement of 20.80% over Average" (§2); no tokens, dollars or seconds reported (§5.2.2 mentions the trade-off only qualitatively).

Derived (mine): selected minus Average / Oracle minus Average = Gemini 69%, Claude 58%, GPT-4.1 66%, Mixture 55%, at N=3. Selection gains of 3 to 9 points over the average candidate.

## Relevance
Shows a read-the-code selector recovers more of the oracle gap at N=3 than the execution-free numbers elsewhere, but with a strong model and agentic reading of the repo; Average is presumably the mean single-candidate rate (definition not given in the extraction). The Trae product-note claim that selector accuracy peaks at 5 to 6 candidates (in `research/2026-10-28-literature-review.md` §4) is NOT in this paper as I read it.

Transfers to our constraints: partly. Claude 3.7 / Gemini 2.5 Pro / GPT-4.1, whole ensembles of full agent runs, no cap. Candidate generation cost (3 full agent runs) is the part we cannot pay.

## Ideas to test
- (mine) Pruning before selection removes 28.9% of candidates and is worth +3.4 to +5.6 points (Table 2 text); on our side, cheap pruning is "drop candidates that fail `python -m py_compile` or touch test files"; claim: at least 1 in 8 dev-48 candidate patches is invalid under this rule.

## Caveats
Selector model unstated; N=3 only in the table; frontier models; Pass@1 over three runs.
