Read through the fetch tool on 2026-11-05 (§4.1, §4.2, §5.1.1, Appendix B). Only the values in the quotes below.

## Takeaway
A trained 32B outcome verifier lifts OpenHands from 20.6@1 to 29.8@8 and 32.0@16 on Verified; the oracle is 37.8@8 and 42.8@16. This is a trained-verifier ceiling, not a recipe for us.

## Method
- Base models Qwen-2.5-Coder-Instruct 7B, 14B, 32B; OpenHands CodeActAgent 2.1 scaffold (§4.1, §4.2).
- Verifier trained on trajectories sampled from SWE-Gym with test-suite outcomes (§5.1).
- Budget: "100 interaction turns or the base LM's 32k context" (App. B.2); successful trajectories about 19 turns and 19,000 tokens (§4.2).

Derived (mine): verifier closes (29.8-20.6)/(37.8-20.6) = 53% of the gap at k=8 and (32.0-20.6)/(42.8-20.6) = 51% at k=16; doubling k from 8 to 16 adds 2.2 points selected and 5.0 oracle.

## Relevance
Gain versus N: most of the selected gain arrives by k=8. Transfers to our constraints: no, as a recipe (trained verifier, 8 to 16 full trajectories). It supports one design number: a selector that recovers about half the oracle gap is the upper end of what the published literature shows.

## Ideas to test
- (mine) Claim: the union-of-arms ceiling on dev-48 (EXP-034 Q1) times 0.5 is the selector's realistic gain; if union minus best arm is below 8 tasks, expected best-of-N gain is below the 4-task MDE.

## Caveats
Trained verifier, large k, 32k context cap (same as ours) but 100 turns.
