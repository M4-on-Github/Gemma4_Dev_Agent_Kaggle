## Takeaway
The only orchestrator-with-sub-agents paper I found with a SWE-bench Verified table against several single-loop scaffolds on the same models. Result is mixed: the orchestrator beats the single loops with Gemini-3-Flash but not with DeepSeek-V3.2, and the single-loop scaffolds themselves differ by up to 36 points on the same model, so "orchestrator versus single loop" depends on which single loop is chosen.

## Method
- The orchestrator "never directly takes environment actions"; its action space is Delegate(Phi) and Finish(y), where a sub-agent is a 4-tuple (instruction, context, tools, model) (§3.2). Whether sub-agents use the orchestrator's model in the main rows is not stated in the extraction; one variant mixes models (Table 3).
- SWE-Bench Verified evaluated on 100 of 500 tasks; Terminal-Bench on 70 of 89 (Appendix A.1).

## Relevance
Part B. Handoff context matters more than the delegation itself (curated beats both none and full on GAIA, +10 to +12 points). Against Mini-SWE with DeepSeek-V3.2 (84) the orchestrator loses 8 points on SWE-V.

Transfers to our constraints: partly. Gemini-3-Flash / DeepSeek-V3.2 class models, no wall-clock cap, 100 SWE tasks, so one task is one point and the CI is about +/-9.

## Ideas to test
- (mine) Claim: the handoff message is the failure surface: in an `agent_tool` worker design, the fraction of delegations whose instruction omits the file path or the error text that the root had in its context exceeds 20%; measure by diffing the worker's first user message against the root's last 3 observations in the traces.

## Caveats
Pass@1 differences of under 10 points on n=100 are within noise; main-row sub-agent models unclear.
