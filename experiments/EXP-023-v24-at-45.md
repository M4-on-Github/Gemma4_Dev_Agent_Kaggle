## 3. The confound, stated before the score, because it is the main threat to reading this at all

**We are not testing their bundle. We are testing their bundle starved of ~44% of its time budget.**

Their 28-call cap at 8 minutes allows ~17.1 s per call. At 4.5 minutes it allows ~9.6 s. Measured on our own
paired smoke-8 cohort, `thinking_budget: 4096` costs **~14.0 s per call** (E0 at thinking 256 averaged 26.1
calls; EXP-020 at 4096 averaged 21.1 — paired −5.0, 5 of 8 tasks down). At ~14 s/call a 4.5-minute cap buys
**~19 calls**, so:

* for **them**, 28 calls was the binding constraint and 8 minutes was slack;
* for **us**, time binds at ~19 calls and **their 28-call cap is never reached**.

E1 is therefore not a small deviation. It removes the resource their prompt is written around — a prompt that
tells the agent to have edited by call 12 of 28, which under our cap is call 12 of ~19. **A null result cannot
retire their bundle**, and §4 is written to respect that.

**Power, stated in advance.** This is a package test of five simultaneous differences (prompt 11,228 → 3,164 B,
calls 64 → 28, turns 96 → 80, timeout 300 → 240, agent name `swe_coder` → `swe_agent`), so it is well powered
for a large effect and near-powerless below ~4 tasks. If the bundle's true θ for us is ~10, P(k ≥ 11) ≈ 0.47;
if it is 6.67 (our pooled rate, i.e. the bundle does nothing for us), P(k ≥ 11) ≈ 0.015. **A hit is strong
evidence; a miss cannot distinguish "their bundle does not transfer" from "we removed the time it needs."**

## 5. What this does and does not license

**Licenses:** a yes/no on whether an externally validated bundle, run inside our gate, beats our own tree by
more than our MDE. Adoption of the lineage on k ≥ 11.

**Does not license:** any claim about *which* component matters; any claim that their prompt is better than
ours; any retirement of the v24 lineage on a null (§3); any inference about the private half, where the
selection component of their 0.24 does not carry.

**Retraction carried forward.** My own binomial dispersion null in `submissions.md` rows 5 and 7 was wrong.
With heterogeneous task difficulty the variance is `Σ pᵢ(1−pᵢ)`, strictly below `N·p̄(1−p̄)` unless every task is
equally hard — so the underdispersion I reported was **mechanical, not evidence**. The measured φ = 0.500
(Fisher p = 0.0023, OR 13.6, on the E0 / EXP-003 resolved-set overlap: 6 shared of 11 and 9) confirms exactly
that heterogeneity. At that φ the expected variance is ≈2.94 against observed 2.267, P ≈ 0.47 — entirely
ordinary. The surviving claim is only that **nothing we have built so far is distinguishable**, not that
configuration cannot matter. `plan.md:302-306` has a related defect: McNemar tests marginal symmetry, so it
does not measure "churn". EXP-022 R1–R11 carries the rest; this one was mine.
